# Research notes: why there were fewer spotted lanternflies in 2026

Compiled 2026-11-15. Pages listed under "Read" were fetched and read. Pages listed under "Search only" were seen only as search-result summaries.

## Bottom line

The drop in 2026 was widely reported around Philadelphia, around Pittsburgh and in Virginia's Shenandoah Valley. None of those reports had standardized counts. The experts quoted mostly say the cause is a mix and that nobody knows for sure. In 2024, Penn State's Brian Walsh said: "It's all guesses at this point. We're not sure, but it's probably a mix of all of those."

## Other useful facts

- **Outlook.** NYU's Kristin Winchell: "Will they swarm again like they did a couple of years ago. Probably not. But are they going away? No." Arlington Parks' Alonso Abugattas expects "a couple more years" of build-up there before numbers stabilize ([FOX 5 DC](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/spotted-lanternfly-population-may-dwindle-coming-years)).
- **Biological control.** USDA's candidate egg parasitoid, *Anastatus orientalis*, was judged too polyphagous for US release (*Biological Control*, Jan 2025). The nymph parasitoid *Dryinus sinicus* was still in host-specificity testing. As of Aug 2025, no agent had been approved for release ([URI Biocontrol Lab](https://web.uri.edu/biocontrol/spotted-lanternfly-biocontrol/)).
- **Penn State Extension guidance** ([guide](https://extension.psu.edu/spotted-lanternfly-management-guide); taken from an automated summary of a long page, so check against the page):
  - Scrape egg masses into alcohol. Fewer than 2% of egg masses sit low enough to reach, and the guide advises against ladders.
  - Circle traps are recommended. Sticky bands are not, because they catch other animals.
  - Kill tree of heaven with herbicide so it does not resprout.
  - Use insecticides only after scouting, and never on blooming plants.
  - Lanternflies are not known to kill plants except grapevines, tree of heaven and black walnut saplings.
