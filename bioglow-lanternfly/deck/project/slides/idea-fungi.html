Idea 3 of 6

Fungus diseases

What people say

Two fungi that already live here can infect lanternflies and wipe out a whole local outbreak.

What we found

In October 2018, near Reading, Pennsylvania, Cornell scientists found an outbreak that had collapsed. The fungi Batkoa major and Beauveria bassiana had killed the lanternflies.

A 2021–2023 survey found fungi caused only 6.7% mortality in total. A summer drought may be why.

Our question for you

Do fungus outbreaks happen often enough to change the numbers from year to year?

Do they depend on wet weather?

Sources: Clifton et al., PNAS (2019); Hajek, Everest and Clifton, Insects (2023)

The 2018 study was at Angora Fruit Farm near Reading, Pennsylvania. On tree trunks, 97 percent of the dead adults had been killed by Batkoa major. The scientists wrote that the future will show whether these fungi drive boom and bust cycles or keep numbers low. The later survey, in Pennsylvania and New York, found 15 more kinds of fungi infecting lanternflies, but infection was low. The authors said the 2022 summer drought probably played a part, because fungi spread better when it is wet. This connects to the weather idea: this summer was hot and dry in some places, which would be bad for fungi.
