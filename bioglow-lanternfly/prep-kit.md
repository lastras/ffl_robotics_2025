# Lanternfly Interview: Team Prep Kit

Nov 15, 2026 · Live version: https://claude.ai/artifact/LbsfxyHLtBTsjkNgoRGJWA

## How to use this kit

For any fact on any slide, every team member should be able to say where it came from and what it does and does not prove. The deck says “we read news stories and science studies,” so an expert may ask any of you about any slide.

The kit takes three meetings of about 45 minutes each, plus a little homework:

1. **Meeting 1: read.** Each kid or pair takes one of the six ideas, reads its sources and fills in its fact card. Everyone learns the basics and the vocabulary.
2. **Meeting 2: our data and our thinking.** Do the “Our own data” tasks, fill in the blue blanks in the deck and talk through “Thinking like a scientist.”
3. **Meeting 3: practice.** An adult plays the expert and asks the practice questions. Make the fixes listed in “Fix before the interview.”

During the interview, the owner of each idea presents that slide and answers the first question about it. Anyone can help. It is fine to say “we read that in a news story, not in the study itself.”

## What everyone must know

Every team member should be able to explain slide 3 and these words without looking.

**The basics.** The spotted lanternfly came from Asia and was first found in the United States in 2014, in southeastern Pennsylvania. By 2025 it was in at least 18 states. It goes through egg, nymph and adult stages: young nymphs are black with white spots, older nymphs turn red, and adults have the spotted wings. One generation grows up each year. Adults die in the fall, and only the egg masses, with 30 to 50 eggs each, live through the winter. It drinks plant sap, and grapevines suffer the most.

**Find it yourself:** in the Penn State guide, look up the months when eggs hatch and when adults appear. Write them here: ______

| Word | What it means | Where it comes up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invasive species | A living thing from somewhere else that spreads and causes harm | The lanternfly, and tree of heaven too |
| Host plant | A plant an insect feeds or lives on | Tree of heaven is its favorite host |
| Nymph | A young insect that has no wings yet | The black and red stages |
| Egg mass | A group of eggs laid together and covered in a mud-like coating | What we scrape in winter |
| Predator | An animal that hunts and eats other animals | Mantises, spiders, birds |
| Parasitoid | An insect that lays its eggs in or on another insect, which then dies | The wasp scientists are testing |
| Pathogen | A germ, such as a fungus, that causes disease | Batkoa and Beauveria |
| Boom and bust | A population that grows very fast, then crashes | Idea 1 |
| Citizen science | Ordinary people collecting data that scientists use | iNaturalist photos |
| Biological control | Using a pest’s natural enemies to control it | Predators, fungi, the wasp |
| Quarantine | Rules that stop people from moving a pest to new places | Checking cars before a trip |

## Thinking like a scientist

Experts will be most impressed if the team can say what each piece of evidence does not prove. These five rules cover almost every slide.

| Rule | Say it like this | Where it shows up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A story is not a count | “People noticed fewer, but nobody counted the same way every year, so we can’t say how many fewer.” | Slide 2, idea 4 |
| A cage is not the outdoors | “Predators ate lanternflies in cages. That shows they can, not that they eat enough outside to matter.” | Idea 2 |
| One place is not everywhere | “The fungus wiped out one outbreak near Reading in 2018. We don’t know how often that happens.” | Ideas 3 and 5 |
| A pattern is not a cause | “Boom and bust tells us what happens, not why it happens.” | Idea 1 |
| Happening together is not proof | “The preserve removed tree of heaven and saw fewer lanternflies, but there were fewer everywhere this year.” | Idea 5 |

**Our ratings on slide 12.** Strong means scientists measured it over many years. Medium means there is a real study, but only in cages, in one place, or it explains the drop only indirectly. Weak means people said it, but we found nobody who measured it. Weak does not mean wrong. The team should talk through each rating and change any it disagrees with.

## Practice interview

An adult plays the expert and asks these questions in any order. A good answer is short, names its source, and says what we don’t know.

| # | The expert asks | A good answer includes | Who answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | How do you know there are fewer, and not just that you noticed fewer? | We didn’t count. Other places reported the same thing. Our iNaturalist numbers, if we have them. That is why counting is our first protocol. | |
| 2 | Did you read the studies themselves, or news stories about them? | Exactly which ones we read in full, and which only as a summary or news story | |
| 3 | Isn’t iNaturalist just counting how many people take photos? | Yes, that is a weakness. It is still the only data that covers many places over many years. It shows a pattern better than an exact number. | |
| 4 | If boom and bust happens on its own, why do anything? | Lanternflies still hurt grapevines, and slowing the spread protects new places. A Penn State educator said you don’t have to kill every single one yourself. | |
| 5 | What is the difference between a predator and a parasitoid? | A predator eats many prey. A parasitoid grows up inside one host, which dies. | |
| 6 | Why would a dry summer matter for fungi? | Fungi spread better when it is wet. The survey authors thought the 2022 drought kept infections low. | |
| 7 | Why not just cut down all the tree of heaven? | It sprouts back from the roots unless an adult uses herbicide. Lanternflies also feed on many other plants. | |
| 8 | Why not use sticky bands? | They catch birds and other animals. Penn State recommends circle traps instead. | |
| 9 | Do lanternflies kill trees? | Penn State says they are not known to kill plants except grapevines, tree of heaven and young black walnut trees. | |
| 10 | Why is weather rated weak when everyone talks about it? | Explain our rating rules: people said it, but nobody measured it. Weak does not mean wrong. | |
| 11 | Which explanation do you think matters most? | The answer the team agreed on beforehand, and why | |
| 12 | What would change your mind? | For example: if numbers jump back up next year after a mild winter, weather mattered more than we thought. | |

**When you don’t know, say one of these:**

- “We don’t know yet. That is one of the things we hoped to ask you.”
- “We read that in a news story, not in the study itself.”
- “Where could we read more about that?”
- “Could you say that again more simply? We want to get it right in our notes.”
